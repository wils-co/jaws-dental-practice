All-on-4 dental implants

All-on-4 dental implants

All-on-4 is a way to replace a full set of failing or missing teeth in one arch using four implants and a fixed bridge. It is a large treatment. It is not a clean-and-polish, and it is not the only way to rebuild a mouth. At Smiles by Design in Wheelers Hill, Dr Julia Nguyen will tell you if this conversation even applies before anyone talks about a date.

Who places the implants, and where the surgery happens, is confirmed after that assessment. This site will not describe an in-house theatre we do not have.

What it is

“All-on-4” is a planning idea: usually four implants in one jaw, angled where bone is better, holding a full-arch bridge. Some people need more than four fixtures. Some people are better with implants in a different pattern, with a denture, or with treating and keeping remaining teeth.

Teeth that are still in the mouth may need to come out as part of the same plan. A temporary bridge is often worn while the implants heal. The longer-term bridge is a later stage, not the same afternoon as a promise.

This is full-mouth rehabilitation territory. It deserves the same caution you would give any hospital-level decision, even when it happens in a dental chair.

Who it may suit

This may be worth discussing if:

  • Most teeth in one jaw are already missing, or cannot reasonably be kept
  • A loose full denture is the alternative you are trying to move away from
  • You understand it is surgery plus months of healing, reviews, and a bridge that will still need repairs over its life
  • Medical history, bone, and gums can be worked up properly first

It may be a poor fit if you want “new teeth tomorrow” with no healing story, if active gum disease or decay in the other jaw hasn’t been faced, if you cannot manage the cleaning a full-arch bridge needs, or if a smaller plan (a few implants, a partial, keeping some teeth) would do.

A webpage cannot tell you which camp you are in.

What happens

  1. Consult. What is failing, what you want to be able to eat and say, and whether All-on-4 is even a candidate idea. Other full-arch options get named in this visit, not after a deposit.
  2. Records. Photos, scans, X-rays, and often a 3D scan. Medical history in writing. Sometimes a specialist opinion (surgery, gums, or medicine) before anyone is booked.
  3. Written plan. How many implants, which jaw, what happens to remaining teeth, whose surgery it is, the temporary and the later bridge, risks, and a guide cost. Take it home. Get a second opinion if you want one.
  4. Surgery day. Remaining teeth in that plan are removed if that was agreed. Implants are placed. A temporary bridge is fitted when the plan says it can be. You go home the same day in most dental settings; you still need someone with you, and you will be swollen.
  5. Healing. Weeks to months. Diet is limited. The temporary can loosen or break. Implants can fail in this window.
  6. The longer-term bridge. Once the fixtures are stable enough, the more durable bridge is made and fitted. Bite, speech, and cleaning space get time. Then recalls, forever.

Where this is done: Consult, records, the written plan, and the restorative stages belong with Julia at Smiles by Design unless she tells you otherwise. Surgical placement, and any hospital or day-surgery setting, is confirmed at consult. There is no in-house general anaesthetic at this practice. If you need to be fully asleep, that is a referral, and it changes the pathway.

Risks and recovery

This is major dental surgery. Things can go well and still be sore. Things can also go badly.

Risks can include:

  • Pain, swelling, bruising, bleeding, and difficulty opening the mouth
  • Infection
  • One or more implants failing, which can mean a remake, extra fixtures, or a different kind of teeth
  • Nerve injury (altered feeling in lip, chin, or tongue)
  • Sinus complications in the upper jaw
  • Fracture or loosening of the temporary or the longer-term bridge, including screws and teeth on the bridge
  • Speech changes, spit-catching, or a lisp while you adapt
  • Food trapping and gum inflammation under the bridge if cleaning is not kept up
  • Not being able to wear the temporary as planned, and spending time in a denture instead
  • The need to treat the other jaw, or to accept that this does not make the rest of the mouth simple

Recovery is not a weekend. Swelling often peaks over a few days. Soft foods last longer than people hope. Time off work is common. Call if swelling or pain is worsening, if bleeding won’t settle, if you have a fever, numbness that isn’t fading, or a bridge or implant that feels loose.

Treatment risks

Other options

  • Keep and restore remaining teeth, if they can be kept
  • A conventional full denture
  • An implant-retained denture (snaps on, still comes out)
  • More than four implants, or a staged plan instead of one surgical day
  • Single-tooth or several-tooth implants if you do not actually need a full arch
  • A second opinion, including from an oral surgeon or prosthodontist

Full-mouth work is easy to oversell. If a smaller plan is honest, that is the plan.

Aftercare

Soft food, no smoking, cleaning around the bridge as shown (usually a water flosser and specific brushes, not “just brush as normal”), and the reviews you were given. Don’t use the temporary as if it were finished teeth. Don’t ignore a rattle or a sore spot under the bridge.

Long term, the bridge will wear. Teeth on it chip. Screws can loosen. That is maintenance, not a surprise, and it has a cost.

FAQs

Does it hurt?

You should be numb for surgery. Afterwards it is sore, swollen, and tiring. We will not call it pain-free. If you need to be fully asleep, that is not something we do in-house.

Will I have teeth the same day?

Sometimes a temporary bridge is fitted on the day of surgery. Sometimes it isn’t, because the bone, the bite, or the healing won’t allow it. “Teeth in a day” as a promise is not how this page is written. Your plan will say which version you are having.

What is All-on-4?

A planning idea: usually four implants in one jaw holding a full-arch bridge. Some people need more than four fixtures. Some people are better with a denture, with several single implants, or with keeping teeth. The longer explanation is in “What it is” above.

How much is All-on-4?

Not a single-tooth implant price. It varies with grafts, whose surgery it is, the temporary, the final bridge, and whether one jaw or both are involved. A full-arch figure only exists as a written plan after an exam. No number on this site is a quote.

All-on-4 vs single implants or a denture?

Single dental implants replace one tooth, or a few, without taking a whole arch. A denture comes out. All-on-4 is a fixed full-arch bridge on a small number of implants. It is more surgery than a denture, and more of a mouth-rebuild than one implant. Julia will say which conversation you are actually in.
