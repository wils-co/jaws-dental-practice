Treatment risks & clinical disclosures

Under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law (AHPRA Section 133) and the Dental Board of Australia guidelines, all surgical, invasive, and irreversible dental treatments carry inherent clinical risks. Before proceeding with treatment, you should consider seeking an independent opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

Clinical Disclosures by Procedure Category

Dental Implants & Full-Arch Surgical Rehabilitation (All-on-4)

Dental implant placement is an invasive surgical procedure. Risks include:

  • Osseointegration Failure: In approximately 2–5% of clinical cases, the titanium fixture fails to fuse with jawbone, requiring removal, a 3-month healing period, and reimplantation.
  • Infection & Peri-Implantitis: Bacterial colonisation can lead to progressive bone loss around an implant without rigorous daily cleaning, flossing, and professional hygiene visits.
  • Nerve Irritation: Lower jaw placement carries a rare risk of temporary or permanent tingling, numbness, or altered sensation in the lower lip or chin.
  • Sinus Complications: Upper jaw placement may require bone grafting or sinus lifting to avoid penetrating sinus cavities.
Porcelain & Composite Veneers

Veneers are cosmetic facings bonded to natural teeth. Important considerations include:

  • Irreversible Enamel Removal: Porcelain veneers usually require irreversible reduction of 0.3 to 0.7 mm of healthy tooth enamel. Once a tooth is prepared, it will always require a restoration.
  • Post-Operative Sensitivity: Mild to moderate temperature sensitivity (cold and hot) is common for 1 to 2 weeks following preparation.
  • Fracture & Chipping: Ceramic is brittle under heavy biting loads. Patients with bruxism (clenching or grinding) must wear a custom night splint to protect their investment.
  • Eventual Replacement: Veneers do not last a lifetime. Average lifespans range from 10 to 15+ years for porcelain, and 4 to 7 years for composite, after which replacement is necessary.
Invisalign & Orthodontics

Moving teeth through bone remodelling involves specific orthodontic considerations:

  • Biological Relapse: Teeth have a natural biological tendency to drift back toward their original positions throughout life. Nighttime wear of retainers is mandatory forever.
  • Compliance Dependency: Aligners must be worn 20 to 22 hours daily. Inconsistent wear leads to tracking failure and extended treatment times.
  • Enamel Interproximal Reduction (IPR): Mild tooth polishing (0.2–0.5 mm) may be required between crowded teeth to create alignment space without extractions.
Local Anaesthesia & Pain Management

Local anaesthetic is used so the procedure itself is comfortable — you may still feel pressure or movement. Considerations include:

  • Temporary Facial Numbness: Numbness typically lasts 2 to 4 hours. Take care to avoid biting cheeks, lips, or tongue while numb.
  • Injection Site Bruising: Mild soreness or localized hematoma at the injection site is uncommon but self-resolves within days.
