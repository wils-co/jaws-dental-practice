Porcelain and composite veneers

Porcelain and composite veneers

Veneers are thin coverings bonded to the front of teeth. They can change colour, shape, or small chips. They usually mean drilling enamel, they are not reversible, and they are not the only option. At Smiles by Design in Wheelers Hill, Dr Julia Nguyen will say whether veneers, bonding, whitening, aligners, or leaving the teeth is the honest list.

What it is

A veneer is a custom facing, often porcelain, sometimes composite (a tooth-coloured filling material shaped on the tooth). Porcelain is made in a lab. Composite is usually built in the chair. They are different treatments in cost, strength, repair, and how much tooth is prepared.

Most porcelain veneers need some enamel removed so the facing has room. That enamel does not grow back. If a veneer fails later, the tooth still needs a covering. That is the trade you are making.

Veneers do not straighten roots. If the problem is position, Invisalign or another orthodontic path may be the smaller lie.

Who it may suit

This may be worth discussing if:

  • You have local colour, shape, or chip issues that whitening or a small bond won’t solve
  • The teeth in question have enough sound structure
  • You understand future repairs, replacements, and the way gums and edges age around a facing

It may be a poor fit if you want a “full new smile” as a product, if the bite is heavy and likely to snap facings, if gums are inflamed, if you grind and won’t wear a splint, or if the real issue is crowding. Heavily restored teeth sometimes need crowns instead, which is a bigger reduction.

Julia will assess this in person. Photos from someone else are not a plan.

What happens

  1. Consult. Which teeth, what bothers you, and whether a less destructive option exists. Risks, including irreversibility, sit here.
  2. Records. Photos, a scan or impressions, and a trial (mock-up or temporary shape) when it helps you decide. A trial is not a guarantee the final will match a picture.
  3. Written plan. Material, how many teeth, how much preparation, a guide cost, what happens if a veneer chips, and whether a night splint is part of the deal. Take it home.
  4. Preparation and temporaries (porcelain). Teeth are prepared. Temporaries can be sensitive, can come off, and will not look like the lab work.
  5. Fit. The veneers are tried in, bonded, and the bite is checked. Shade is agreed in the room, in ordinary light, not under a filter.
  6. Review. Cleaning edges, bite, and a splint if that was planned.

Where this is done: At this practice, with Julia, unless she refers a particular stage. There is no in-house milling promise (no CEREC claim). Lab work goes to a lab.

Risks and recovery

Veneers can look good and still be a problem later.

Risks can include:

  • Irreversible loss of enamel
  • Sensitivity to cold, sometimes lasting, sometimes meaning a later root canal (uncommon, real)
  • Chips, cracks, debonding, or wear of the opposing teeth
  • Edges that pick up stain, or a gum line that recedes and shows a margin
  • Colour that does not match neighbouring teeth, especially as natural teeth keep changing
  • Need to treat decay or a failing core underneath
  • Temporaries breaking or coming off before the fit visit

Recovery is usually a sensitive few days, not a surgical swelling story. Call if a temporary or a veneer comes off, if pain is getting worse, or if a bite feels high.

Treatment risks

Other options

  • Whitening only, if colour is the issue and the teeth are otherwise fine
  • Composite bonding, which can be more conservative and also stains and chips
  • Aligners, if position is the issue
  • Crowns, if the tooth is already heavily broken down
  • Do nothing

A row of ten veneers is not automatically better than treating two teeth. We will not upsell a “smile makeover” package on this page.

Aftercare

Treat them like teeth that cannot be taken for granted: a night splint if you grind, no bottle-tops, careful with stain at the edges, and ordinary gum cleaning. They are not stain-proof forever. They are not a reason to skip checks.

FAQs

Does it hurt?

Preparation is done with anaesthetic. Afterwards, sensitivity is common. We don’t call it pain-free.

How much are veneers?

Per tooth, and it depends on porcelain vs composite, how many teeth, and whether a splint or gum work is in the plan. The written plan is the quote. We do not bulk bill. Cost stays on this page.

How much do veneers cost in Australia?

Same answer. National “from” prices are not ours. Nothing on this page is a quote for Melbourne until it is in your plan.

Porcelain vs composite veneers?

Porcelain is usually made in a lab and needs more preparation. Composite is usually built in the chair, is often more conservative, and is easier to repair and easier to stain. They are different treatments in cost, strength, and how they fail. Julia will say which (if either) belongs on the tooth in front of her. This page is about dental veneers, not brick veneer.

How long do veneers last?

Years, not a lifetime, and not the same number for everyone. Bite, grinding, and how much tooth was prepared all change that. A single “typical” lifespan on a billboard is marketing. Ask what we would do when (not if) one needs a remake, and what that costs.

Can I see before and after photos?

Not as a gallery that implies your result. If Julia later supplies individual cases, they will sit with the risks and with “this is one person”. Day one, there isn’t a results menu.
