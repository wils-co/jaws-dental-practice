## What counts as a dental emergency here

Call us if:

- You have toothache that is not settling with what you’ve already tried
- A tooth or filling has broken, cracked, or come out
- A crown or veneer has come off
- You have swelling in the gum or face that you think is from a tooth
- You’ve knocked a tooth, or a tooth is loose after a hit
- A socket is still bleeding or in severe pain after an extraction elsewhere

Lost whitening trays, a cosmetic chip you can live with until next week, and a routine clean do not need emergency time. Book an ordinary visit for those.

## What happens when you call

1. **Tell us what’s going on.** Pain, swelling, trauma, and whether you can eat or sleep. If you are a new patient, say so.
2. **We find a time.** Same day when we can. Not always. If we cannot see you safely, we’ll say that and tell you where else to go.
3. **Today’s job is to make it safe and comfortable enough.** That might be an exam, an X-ray, a dressing, trimming a sharp edge, putting a crown back on with temporary cement, opening a tooth to relieve pressure, or antibiotics when they are actually indicated (they are not a toothache cure).
4. **The lasting fix is often a second visit.** A root canal, an extraction, an implant conversation, or a new crown is planned, costed, and booked. We will not start that on a squeezed emergency slot unless it is the only safe option and you’ve agreed.

**Where this is done:** At this practice, with Julia, during opening hours. [hours TBD]. There is no after-hours on-call promised on this page until one exists. There is no in-house general anaesthetic. If you need care asleep, or you need a hospital, we refer.

## Risks and recovery

Emergency visits still have risks: the temporary fix can fail before the next appointment; numbness from anaesthetic; a dressing can come out; antibiotics can cause side effects and they don’t replace treatment; an extraction, if it becomes necessary today, has its own bleeding and dry-socket risks.

If swelling, pain, or breathing gets worse after you leave, do not wait for your review. Call us, or if it is serious, 000.

## Other options

- Your usual dentist, if you have one and they can see you sooner
- A hospital emergency department for spreading swelling, trauma to the face, or medical red flags
- After-hours dental services in Melbourne if we are closed and you cannot wait

## Aftercare

You’ll leave with what to eat, what to avoid chewing on, and whether you need a prescription. Temporary cement and dressings are temporary. Chew on the other side. If a piece comes out, keep it and call.

## FAQs

**What are your emergency hours?**
We see emergencies during ordinary opening hours when the diary allows. Hours are still to be published. There is no 24/7 on-call promised on this page until one exists. If we are closed and you cannot wait, use an after-hours dental service or, for spreading swelling or breathing trouble, 000.

**Are you open Sunday?**
Sunday hours are not promised on launch. If that changes, it will be on this page and in the footer NAP. Do not assume a Sunday clinic.

**How much does an emergency visit cost?**
Guide fees for an emergency consult will sit on the [Fees](/fees) page when Julia has set them. Any treatment beyond the consult is quoted before we do it, unless it is urgent and you’ve agreed to proceed. We do not bulk bill.

**Do I need to be a patient already?**
No. Say you’re new when you call.

**Can you prescribe something without seeing me?**
Usually no. Medicines for dental pain and infection need a proper look. Don’t wait on an old leftover antibiotic.

**What if a tooth was knocked out?**
If it is a permanent tooth, pick it up by the crown (not the root), sit it in milk or in your own saliva, and call immediately. Don’t scrub it. Time matters. We’ll tell you on the phone whether to come here or go straight to hospital.
