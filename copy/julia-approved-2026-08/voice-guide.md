# Voice guide — Dr Julia Nguyen

The reference document for anything written in her name: site copy, LinkedIn, education modules, social, email. **Agents writing marketing must check output against this file.** Without it, everything defaults to generic dental copy — the exact register she has repeatedly told us to strip out.

Source: voice interview 8–9 Aug 2026 (`voice-interview-raw.md`). Every principle below traces to something she actually said.

---

## 1. The positioning

**She is the dentist whose work you don't have to think about again.**

Durability — clinical and relational. Not a pleasant appointment. This is a claim about *time*, unlike the per-visit claims competitors make (gentle, painless, on schedule), and it can only be earned by not accumulating failures behind you.

Her own evidence for it, in her words: *"revive them, and replace them if they were lost"* · *"I still see his whole family"* · *"consistent in her quality and her demeanor… and that they've never had a problem."*

## 2. The method underneath it

**She reduces what a patient has to take on faith.**

This is the through-line in every answer she gave, and it is the thing to reach for when a piece of copy feels aimless:

| What she does | Instead of |
|---|---|
| Says the price herself, in the room | Handing the patient to reception to meet the number at a public counter |
| Quotes appointments and duration, not just dollars | Quoting money only, and losing them halfway through |
| Shows scan data across time | "We'll keep an eye on it" |
| Ends the opener on a question so they talk first | Explaining before knowing who she's explaining to |
| Long version or short version, depending on the patient | One fixed script |

Her line: *"it also gives patients visual of what's happening, without relying on what the dentist is saying."*

## 3. Who she's for

For: people who would have chosen proper treatment if anyone had properly explained it to them. Not for: people who've had it explained and still want the patch — continual patching, compromised treatment, not following advice, unrealistic expectations, no time and expecting a magic fix.

**The dividing line is whether the problem was ever properly put to them — not wealth, not dental IQ.** Keep it clinical. Never write demographics.

## 4. Register

**Measured, professional, warm without gushing. Third person for bio and formal copy.** Full sentences, properly built — not clipped. Concrete nouns. Lead with the fact, not the feeling.

Her instruction, verbatim: **"make it sound less fluffy."**

**Important correction (9 Aug 2026).** A first draft read this as *blunt* — short punchy sentences, conversational, first person — and she rejected it as "too crass and not upmarket professional enough." That was a misreading. "Less fluffy" means **not saccharine**; it does not mean terse. She wants the register of a considered professional profile, not a plain-spoken one.

The discipline is not in the sentence length. It is in **replacing generic claims with specific ones**: where a competitor would write "committed to excellence", she has an actual CPD list; where they write "we take time with patients", she has published appointment lengths. Keep the polish. Lose the padding.

Where it comes from: the retired lawyer who listened to her long careful explanation and handed it back as *"so you're telling me…"* in one sentence. Get to the crux. Detail is available underneath for whoever wants it.

**Structure everything as progressive disclosure.** Crux first, then the detail for the ones who need it — because she does exactly this in the chair, and it depends on the patient.

### Banned

Journey. Smile journey. Commitment to excellence. Passionate about smiles. State-of-the-art. Cutting-edge. Family-friendly. Gentle, caring, understanding (as unsupported adjectives). Your smile is our priority. Any sentence that would appear unchanged on a competitor's site.

### Test for any adjective

If a competitor could claim it and no reader could check it, cut it or prove it. "I don't rush patients" is invisible — everyone says it. Publishing appointment lengths is the same claim, checkable, and needs no adjective.

### Rewrite examples

> ❌ Dr Julia is passionate about creating beautiful, healthy smiles that last a lifetime.
> ✅ I do implants, full mouth rehabilitation, and cracked teeth. Work that has to last decades.

> ❌ We take the time to listen to every patient.
> ✅ A new patient exam is 45 minutes. An implant consult is an hour.

> ❌ We use state-of-the-art digital scanning technology.
> ✅ Most dentists say we'll monitor it. How do you monitor something that changes by microns over six months? I scan, and we compare.

> ❌ Committed to conservative, ethical treatment.
> ✅ Just because you can do something doesn't mean you should.

## 5. Her vocabulary

Words and constructions that are hers — use them, don't smooth them out:

- *revive them, and replace them if they were lost*
- *just because you can do it doesn't mean you should*
- *it depends on the patient*
- *the crux of it*
- *a stable choice* / *won't be stable*
- *non-functional* (of a tooth doing no work)
- *doesn't try to sell you things*
- *consistent*

She thinks in measurements and clinical principles, not procedure labels — millimetres of vertical loss, percentage of buccal defect, gIDE Protocol 1/2/3. When writing technical content, mirror that: describe the situation, not the diagnosis name. She explicitly rejected reasoning from "the actual presentation of the tooth."

## 6. Content seeds from her own material

Each of these is hers, none of them appear on competitor sites:

1. **Not every tooth needs saving.** Some teeth aren't doing anything. Patients believe a good dentist saves everything.
2. **Why your dentist might say no.** Patients know about informed consent; almost none know it runs both ways. Frame as *I won't take your money for something that will break* — never doctor-knows-best.
3. **"We'll monitor it" usually means nothing.** The micron argument. Flagship module: two scans six months apart, overlaid.
4. **What it costs in time, not just money.** Appointments and duration published alongside price.
5. **The long version and the short version.** Every explanation on the site available at both depths.

## 7. Hard guardrails

Non-negotiable. These are regulatory, not stylistic.

- **Never "prosthodontist", "specialist", or "specialising in".** Her signed documents say General Dentist and the title is protected under s118. Write *general dentist with a prosthodontic and implant focus* until she confirms otherwise in writing. See `dental-title-unresolved` in memory.
- **No testimonials.** Banned outright, s133. Facts about her practice are fine and stronger anyway: *"I still see his whole family."*
- **No comparative or superiority claims.** Not "first", not "far better than". Other scanners do time-lapse too. Say what it does for the patient.
- **Never criticise another dentist**, named or identifiable. AHPRA bites on disparagement, and a patient reading one dentist run down another concludes dentists are unreliable — not that this one is honest. Make the point about the tooth, never the operator.
- **No unrealistic expectation-setting.** Before/after imagery is heavily conditioned; education content carries no such drag, which is why it's the whole strategy.
- **Nothing that reads as soliciting.** She is an associate at two practices she doesn't own, with a 5km/12-month restraint at BCDC. No booking engine, no "become my patient" during this phase.

## 8. The publication test

**Would she be comfortable if Andrea Manley or Dina Liosis read this page?**

Assume they will — principals google their associates. Anything that fails goes in the private acquisition material, not the site. Ownership ambitions, catchment analysis, and market segmentation never appear in public copy.

---

## 9. Two things she must decide, not us

- **How much of her childhood goes public.** She mentioned growing up in an environment not conducive to thriving. It explains the drive, but she owes a website nothing. The drive shows in the work without the biography. Her call at copy stage — never assumed into a draft.
- **"I stumbled into dentistry."** She says this, and it's inaccurate: she was offered medicine on her science scores and declined it, because she was good with her hands and wanted craft plus medicine. That's a choice. Push back if she reaches for "stumbled" in her own copy.
