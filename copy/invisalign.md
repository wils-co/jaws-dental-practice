## What it is

Aligners are trays you wear most of the day and night, and take out to eat and clean. Each tray is a slightly different shape. Teeth move if you wear them as prescribed, if the plan is suitable, and if nothing in the bite is fighting the movement.

Attachments (small tooth-coloured bumps), polishing between teeth (IPR), and retainers at the end are a normal part of many aligner plans, including Invisalign. People are often surprised by those. They should be in the consult, not a footnote.

This page is about aligner treatment as it relates to Invisalign. Other clear aligners exist. So do braces. Julia will not pick a brand on a webpage.

## Who it may suit

This may be worth discussing if:

- You want to straighten crowding, spacing, or a mild bite problem and you can wear trays 20-plus hours a day
- You can come to reviews, and you will wear retainers when the moving is done
- Your gums and teeth are healthy enough to move, or can be made so first

It may be a poor fit if you want a guarantee of a particular look, if you will not wear the trays, if the bite needs a kind of movement aligners do poorly, or if gum disease or decay needs to go first. Wisdom teeth are not a launch topic here; they only matter if they actually change this plan.

A scan or a photo set is not a treatment promise. Julia has to look.

## What happens

1. **Consult.** What bothers you, what you will wear, medical and dental history. Whether aligners, braces, a small bit of bonding, or leaving it is the honest list.
2. **Records.** Photos, a scan or impressions, and X-rays when they change the plan.
3. **A plan you can refuse.** If treatment is offered, you should see the intended movements, what attachments or IPR would be involved, a time range (not a single “typical” number), risks, and a guide cost. You can say no.
4. **Start.** Trays are issued with wear instructions. You will feel pressure. That is not the same as “pain-free”.
5. **Reviews.** Fit, tracking, and whether the plan needs a refinement (extra trays). Refinements are common. They are not a failure of the patient.
6. **Retainers.** Teeth move back. Retainers are part of the treatment, not an optional extra at the end. How long you wear them is a clinical decision, often a long one.

## Risks and recovery

Aligners are orthodontics. Moving teeth has risks.

Those can include:

- Soreness each time you change trays
- Lisping or extra saliva at the start
- Attachments staining or coming off
- Sore spots on cheeks or tongue
- Teeth not tracking, which can mean extra trays, braces, or stopping
- Gum recession, root shortening (root resorption), or a tooth that becomes more sensitive or needs a root canal (uncommon, real)
- Bite changes that feel worse before they feel better, or that need adjustment
- Relapse if retainers are not worn
- Cavities and gum disease if trays are worn over a dirty mouth

There is no “recovery week” in the implant sense. There is an awkward first few days, then a routine. Call if a tooth is very painful, if an attachment is lost, or if a tray doesn’t seat and you are guessing.

## Other options

- Do nothing, if the bite is stable and you can live with the look
- Braces, including a referral if that is the better mechanic
- Limited treatment (a few teeth), instead of a full arch
- [Veneers](/veneers) or bonding, which change shape and colour without straightening roots. That is a different trade-off, and often a more destructive one if the real issue is position
- A second opinion

## Aftercare

Wear the hours you were given. Clean teeth and trays separately. Don’t drink anything but water with trays in. Don’t wrap trays in a napkin and lose them. When the moving is done, retainers are the treatment.

## FAQs

**Does it hurt?**
Pressure, yes. Sharp pain is not the aim. We don’t describe aligners as pain-free.

**How much does Invisalign cost?**
A full aligner plan is not a whitening price. It depends on how much movement, whether attachments or IPR are involved, refinements, and retainers. The written plan is the quote. Health funds sometimes help with orthodontic items; check waiting periods. We do not bulk bill. Cost stays on this page; there is no separate Invisalign-cost URL.

**How much is Invisalign?**
Same answer. A phone number without an exam is not a fee.

**How long does Invisalign take?**
It depends on the movement, the wear, and whether refinements are needed. Months is the usual scale; a single “12 months” as the typical course is too neat. Your plan will have a range.

**Invisalign vs braces?**
Aligners are trays you take out. Braces are fixed to the teeth. Some bites move more predictably with braces. Some people will not wear trays enough for aligners to work. “Better” is not a word we use. Julia will say which mechanic fits, or whether she refers.

**Is Invisalign better than other aligners?**
“Better” is still not a word we use. Systems differ in how they are planned and who can provide them. Julia will say what she can actually offer you.
